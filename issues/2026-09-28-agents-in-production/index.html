Agents can work for 12 hours. Most companies can't use them yet

Agent capability is doubling roughly every four months. Enterprise adoption is not, and the gap between the two is where this year's winners and write-offs will come from.

TL;DR

  • The length of task a frontier agent can finish on its own has doubled about every 129 days since 2023, from roughly 4 minutes (GPT-4) to about 12 hours (Claude Opus 4.6)1.
  • Adoption is far behind: 62% of organizations are at least experimenting with agents, but only 23% are scaling them anywhere5.
  • Gartner expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to be canceled by the end of 2027. Cost, unclear value and weak risk controls are the reasons, not capability3.

Trends

The capability curve keeps bending up

METR measures agents by the length of task, in human working time, that a model finishes with 50% reliability. On its latest benchmark that time horizon went from about 4 minutes for GPT-4 in March 2023 to about 12 hours for Claude Opus 4.6 in February 20261.

The pace is getting faster. METR's original 2025 study found a doubling time of about seven months over six years2. Its updated data puts the post-2023 doubling time at about 129 days, roughly four months1.

Treat the newest numbers with care. An early Claude Mythos Preview scored above 16 hours, and METR says measurements above 16 hours are unreliable with its current task suite1. The trend is clear. The exact frontier is fuzzy.

Insights

Adoption is wide but shallow

McKinsey's 2025 State of AI survey shows how thinly this spreads inside companies. 88% of organizations use AI in at least one business function, and 62% are at least experimenting with agents. Only 23% are scaling agentic systems anywhere in the business5.

Value is rarer still. Only 39% report any EBIT impact from AI, and about 6% are high performers that attribute more than 5% of EBIT to it5. Those high performers were 2.8x more likely to have redesigned workflows from the ground up5. The model isn't the moat. The process around it is.

News

A cancellation wave is coming

Gartner predicts that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by the end of 2027. It names escalating costs, unclear business value and inadequate risk controls, not model capability3.

Part of the problem is supply. Gartner calls it agent washing: chatbots, RPA and assistants rebranded as agents. It estimates that only about 130 of the thousands of vendors selling agentic AI are the real thing3.

Investment has been cautious from the start. In Gartner's January 2025 poll, only 19% of organizations had made significant investments in agentic AI3.

AI & Tech

What separates the agents that survive

Gartner's reasons for cancellation (cost, value and risk) are the checklist for staying off the list3. On capability, METR's earlier work found that 2025 agents succeeded less than 10% of the time on tasks that take humans more than about four hours2. Long tasks are still where reliability breaks down, so the projects that last are the ones scoped to what agents can reliably do today.

The bar keeps rising. Gartner expects up to 40% of enterprise apps to include task-specific agents by the end of 2026, up from under 5% in 20254. It also predicts that by 2028 at least 15% of day-to-day work decisions will be made autonomously3.

What to watch

  1. Plan for the curve, build for the floor. Design workflows around today's reliable task length, and re-test every quarter as horizons double1.
  2. Tie every agent to one business metric before scaling. Unclear value is one of the three reasons Gartner gives for cancellation3.
  3. Redesign the workflow, not just the tool. High performers were 2.8x more likely to have done so5.
  4. Check vendors' claims. Ask for autonomous task length, evaluation results and failure handling. Most "agents" on the market aren't agents3.
  5. Budget for oversight. Guardrails, audit trails and human escalation are what get an agent past risk review.
