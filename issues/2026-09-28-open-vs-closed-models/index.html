Open models do most of the work. Closed models still get paid

Open-weight AI now handles most developer tokens and runs about four months behind the frontier. It wins on price for some jobs but not all. Here's how to choose.

TL;DR

  • Closed models still hold the top spot: Claude Opus 5.5 scores 58 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, and the best open model, Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6-Pro, scores 4612.
  • The gap is about four months. Open models match what closed models could do a season earlier3.
  • In August, open-weight models ran 56% of tokens on Vercel's AI Gateway but took only 14% of the spend4.

Trends

Who's on top this month

Closed models still lead on raw capability. Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 tops the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at 58. OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, launched September 3, is next at 53, and Meta's proprietary Muse Spark 1.3 follows at 4814.

The open-weight pack is close behind and crowded. Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6-Pro (46), Z AI's GLM-5.3 (45) and Moonshot's Kimi K3 (44) are the strongest models you can download and run yourself2. They score level with SpaceXAI's Grok 4.7 and Alibaba's closed Qwen3.8 Max1.

Google's highest-ranked current model on the index is Gemini 3.8 Flash at 411. The index weighs agentic and knowledge-work tasks heavily, so treat it as one lens, not a verdict.

Insights

The gap is measured in months, not years

Epoch AI finds that since January 2026, the best open-weight models have trailed the best closed models by about four months on its capabilities index. That's slightly more than the three-month lag it measured through October 20253.

Epoch also warns that the figure probably understates the real distance. Open models tend to optimize for public benchmarks, while closed labs hold back their strongest internal systems3.

Mozilla's State of Open Source AI report makes the gap concrete. It fits a lag of about 4.4 months on METR's task-length data: closed models can handle 8 to 12 hour tasks, and open models get there about four months later5. For most everyday work, that gap doesn't matter. For the hardest, longest tasks, it does.

AI & Tech

Open isn't automatically cheaper

Price per token is the wrong number to compare. What matters is cost per task, which depends on both the price and how many tokens a model burns thinking. Among models scoring 44 to 46 on the index, cost per task runs from $0.13 to $5.411.

The cheapest is open: MiMo-V2.6-Pro, at $0.131. But GLM-5.3 and Kimi K3, both open, cost about $2 per task, more than twice the $0.82 of GPT-6 Astra on its low setting, which is closed and scores higher (46)1. Run your own workload through both kinds before assuming that open saves money.

News

Open models win the volume, closed models win the money

On Vercel's AI Gateway, open-weight models went from 7% of tokens in December to 13% in April and 56% in August, the first month they carried the majority. But they accounted for only 14% of spend, and Anthropic alone kept 64%4. Teams send bulk work to cheap open models and pay for closed ones where quality matters.

OpenRouter shows the same pattern. Eight of its top ten models by token volume in August were open-weight, and seven of those eight were built in China5. On August 3, DeepSeek became the first open-model maker to rank first on the platform by requests5.

Deployment is the bottleneck. Developers take open models to production 12 percentage points less often than closed ones, and Mozilla's survey with SlashData traces the gap to tooling, not capability5. Enterprise spending still leans heavily closed: as of December 2025, open-source models had just 11% of enterprise LLM usage, down from 19%6.

Insights

How to choose

Open weights mean you can download the model, host it on your own infrastructure and fine-tune it2. Closed models give you the frontier and a managed service. Most teams will end up using both.

What to watch

  1. Route by difficulty. Send bulk, routine work to open models and escalate to closed ones for the hardest tasks. That's what gateway traffic already shows teams doing4.
  2. Benchmark cost per task, not price per token. Open and closed models at the same score differ by more than 40x in cost1.
  3. Budget for tooling. The main thing keeping open models out of production is tooling, not model quality5.
  4. Re-test every quarter. With a gap of about four months, today's closed-only capability is likely to be available open by early 20273.
  5. Mind where your model comes from. Seven of the eight most-used open models on OpenRouter are Chinese-built, so check the licence and your compliance requirements before deploying5.
